When · Why The census sets off a chain that ends in how much your vote is worth. Tracing it — through Tennessee’s own Baker v. Carr — shows why redistricting is a fight worth having. Cause-and-effect reasoning is TN Practice SSP.05.
Work down the chain: what each step sets in motion.
1
The Census (every 10 years) — what does the constitutional count measure?
2
Apportionment — how are the 435 House seats split among the states?
3
Redistricting — who redraws the lines within a state, and how can gerrymandering tilt them?
4
Baker v. Carr (1962) — how did a Tennessee lawsuit lead to ‘one person, one vote’?
So what? How can drawing lines change who wins without changing a single vote? ____________________________________
Make it work for every student UDL · MTSS

Scaffold

Provide the four steps on cards; students order them, then add one effect each.

Extend

Explain how population shifts to cities changed Tennessee’s representation before Baker v. Carr.

Show it your way

Students may write, say (record), draw, or build their response.
