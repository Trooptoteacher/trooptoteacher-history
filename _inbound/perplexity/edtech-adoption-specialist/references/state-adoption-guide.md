# TDOE State-Level Instructional Materials Adoption Guide

This reference covers the Tennessee Department of Education (TDOE) instructional materials adoption process for vendors seeking state-level approval.

## Overview of TDOE Adoption Process

Tennessee follows a cyclical adoption process for instructional materials. The State Textbook and Instructional Materials Quality Commission (TIMQC) manages the process under TCA § 49-6-2201 et seq.

### Adoption Cycle

- TDOE announces adoption cycles by subject area on a rotating schedule (typically 6-8 year cycles)
- Publishers/vendors submit materials during an open call period
- Materials undergo multiple levels of review
- Approved materials are placed on the state-approved list
- Districts select from the approved list (or may use non-adopted materials with justification)

### Key Entities

| Entity | Role |
|--------|------|
| **TIMQC** (Textbook and Instructional Materials Quality Commission) | Oversees the adoption process, appoints review committees, makes final recommendations |
| **TDOE Office of Academics** | Manages day-to-day adoption logistics and communication |
| **State Review Committees** | Content experts and educators who evaluate submitted materials |
| **State Board of Education** | Provides final approval of the adopted materials list |
| **LEA (Local Education Agency)** | Individual districts that select from adopted materials |

## Submission Requirements

### Eligibility

- Materials must align to current Tennessee Academic Standards for the subject area
- Vendor must be a legally registered entity
- Materials must be available for the full adoption period (typically 6-8 years)
- Digital materials must meet accessibility requirements

### Required Submission Components

1. **Publisher's Affidavit** — Legal declaration of compliance with Tennessee law
2. **Correlations Document** — Standard-by-standard alignment showing where each TN standard is addressed in the materials
3. **Complete Instructional Materials** — Full student and teacher editions (print and/or digital)
4. **Supplementary Materials** — Any ancillary resources included in the adoption
5. **Accessibility Documentation** — VPAT, WCAG compliance statement
6. **Technology Requirements** — System requirements, device compatibility, bandwidth needs
7. **Pricing Information** — Per-student, per-school, and per-district pricing for the adoption period
8. **Professional Development Plan** — Training available for teachers implementing the materials
9. **Privacy and Data Security Documentation** — Privacy policy, DPA template, compliance statements

### Correlations Document — Critical Detail

The correlations document is the most scrutinized component. It must:

- Map every Tennessee Academic Standard to specific locations in the materials (page numbers, lesson numbers, module identifiers)
- Demonstrate primary instruction (not just supplementary mention) for each standard
- Show depth of coverage aligned to the standard's cognitive demand
- For U.S. History: Map to standards US.01 through US.95 with specific content references

**Format Example:**

| TN Standard | Standard Description | Primary Location(s) | Depth of Coverage | Notes |
|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------|-------|
| US.01 | Analyze the transformation of the American economy... | Ch. 1, Lessons 1.2-1.4; Interactive Module 1A | Comprehensive | Full unit coverage with primary sources |
| US.02 | Evaluate the impact of the Transcontinental Railroad... | Ch. 1, Lesson 1.3; Assessment Bank Q12-Q18 | Comprehensive | Includes map analysis activity |

## Review Process

### Phase 1: Administrative Review
- TDOE staff verify all required documents are submitted
- Incomplete submissions are returned without review
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks after submission deadline

### Phase 2: Content Review
- State review committees evaluate materials for:
  - **Standards alignment** — Do materials fully address all Tennessee standards?
  - **Content accuracy** — Is the content factually correct and current?
  - **Pedagogical quality** — Are materials well-designed for effective instruction?
  - **Bias and sensitivity** — Are materials free from bias, stereotypes, and culturally insensitive content?
  - **Age-appropriateness** — Compliance with TN Age-Appropriate Materials Act
  - **Accessibility** — Can all students access the materials?
  - **Technology functionality** — Do digital components work reliably?

### Phase 3: Public Review Period
- Materials are made available for public comment (typically 30-60 days)
- Districts, teachers, parents, and community members can submit feedback
- TDOE publishes submitted materials for review at designated locations

### Phase 4: TIMQC Deliberation
- Commission reviews committee reports and public feedback
- Makes recommendations for the approved list
- May request revisions or additional information from vendors

### Phase 5: State Board Approval
- State Board of Education votes on the final adopted materials list
- Approved materials are published on the TDOE website

## Evaluation Criteria & Scoring

Review committees typically score materials across these dimensions:

### Standards Alignment (Highest Weight — ~40%)
- Every standard addressed with primary instructional content
- Appropriate depth and complexity
- Logical scope and sequence aligned to TN pacing guides
- Assessment opportunities for each standard

### Content Quality (~25%)
- Factual accuracy
- Current and relevant content
- Engaging and rigorous
- Diverse perspectives represented
- Primary source integration

### Instructional Design (~20%)
- Clear learning objectives
- Scaffolded instruction (UDL principles)
- Differentiation opportunities
- Formative and summative assessment integration
- Teacher support materials (answer keys, pacing guides, rubrics)

### Accessibility & Usability (~15%)
- WCAG 2.2 Level AA compliance
- Multiple means of representation, engagement, and action/expression
- Technology reliability and ease of use
- Offline access capabilities (if digital)
- Device compatibility (Chromebook, iPad, desktop)

## Tennessee-Specific Requirements

### Age-Appropriate Materials Act (Public Chapter 744)

All submitted materials must comply with the Tennessee Age-Appropriate Materials Act of 2022:

- No sexually explicit content as defined under TCA § 49-6-2201
- Materials must be appropriate for the designated grade level
- Vendor must certify compliance in the Publisher's Affidavit
- Content review procedures must be documented and available upon request

### Tennessee Academic Standards Alignment

For U.S. History specifically:
- Materials must cover the full scope: Reconstruction through the Modern Era
- Standards US.01 through US.95 must all be addressed
- Content must reflect the approved 11-unit structure:
  1. The Reconstruction Era
  2. Industrialization, Immigration, and Urbanization
  3. The Progressive Era and American Imperialism
  4. World War I and the 1920s
  5. The Great Depression and the New Deal
  6. World War II
  7. The Cold War
  8. The Civil Rights Movement
  9. The Vietnam War Era
  10. Contemporary United States (1970s-2000s)
  11. The Modern Era (2000s-Present)

### Professional Development Requirements

TDOE expects vendors to provide:
- Initial implementation training (minimum 1 day recommended)
- Ongoing support resources (webinars, help desk, documentation)
- Train-the-trainer models for district-level scaling
- Standards alignment training showing how materials map to TN standards

## Timeline & Deadlines

Typical adoption cycle timeline:

| Phase | Typical Timeline |
|-------|-----------------|
| TDOE announces adoption call | 12-18 months before adoption |
| Submission deadline | 6-9 months before adoption |
| Administrative review | 2-4 weeks after deadline |
| Content review | 2-3 months |
| Public review period | 30-60 days |
| TIMQC deliberation | 1-2 months |
| State Board vote | Following regular board meeting |
| Adoption effective date | Beginning of next school year |
| District selection period | 3-6 months after state adoption |

## Tips for Successful State Adoption

1. **Start the correlations document early.** It is the most labor-intensive component and the primary basis for content review.

2. **Engage Tennessee educators in development.** Materials developed with input from TN teachers are stronger in review.

3. **Pilot in Tennessee districts.** Having district implementation data strengthens the submission.

4. **Address all standards explicitly.** Gaps in standards coverage are the most common reason for rejection.

5. **Provide robust teacher materials.** Strong teacher editions, pacing guides, and PD plans significantly improve review scores.

6. **Exceed accessibility minimums.** UDL principles throughout (not just accommodations bolted on) distinguish top-rated materials.

7. **Prepare for public scrutiny.** The public review period can surface concerns about specific content. Anticipate and prepare responses.

8. **Price competitively.** While not a formal scoring criterion, cost-effectiveness influences district selection from the adopted list.

## Non-Adopted Materials Path

Districts may use materials not on the state-adopted list if:

- The LEA board approves the materials
- The materials align to Tennessee Academic Standards
- The selection process follows local policy
- Documentation of the selection rationale is maintained

This "local adoption" path is an alternative for vendors who want to work with specific districts before or instead of pursuing state adoption.
