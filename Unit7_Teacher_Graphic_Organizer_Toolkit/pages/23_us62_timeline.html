When · Why A timeline makes the war’s course visible — and drives home why it’s called a stalemate: after three years and heavy losses, the border barely moved. Chronological reasoning is TN Social Studies Practice SSP.05.
Follow the war’s course from top (earliest) to bottom (latest). Write over the faint notes, then notice the ending: after three years, the border was almost exactly where it started.

Latest · a divided Korea
★ Tennessee Connection. Thousands of Tennesseans served in Korea. Fort Campbell’s 187th Airborne made combat jumps at Sukchon and Munsan‑ni, and Tennessee’s Korean War veterans are memorialized at the War Memorial Building in Nashville.
Make it work for every student UDL · MTSS

Scaffold

Give the five stages on cards; have students sequence them before writing. Ask: where did the tide turn twice?

Extend

Was Korea a win, a loss, or a draw for the U.S.? Use the ending to defend your answer.

Show it your way

Students may write, say the sequence aloud, or map the front line moving up and down Korea.
