**Document purpose.** This playbook explains what has been built, how all the pieces connect, and what every teacher, reviewer, or future maintainer needs to know to implement or evaluate the TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC *U.S. History: Reconstruction to Present* instructional materials. It does not claim state approval, waiver approval, or completion of any item whose resolution depends on an external party.

1. Purpose and Overview

What this course is

U.S. History: Reconstruction to Present is a full-year high school course aligned to the Tennessee Academic Standards for U.S. History (US.01–US.95, current standards and 2024 revised forward-compatibility). It is designed for 11th-grade students in Tennessee public schools and covers the period from 1865 through the present across ten instructional units.

The course is developed and published by TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC (Sean Reynolds, M.Ed., Franklin, Tennessee). It is currently available as supplemental instructional materials under TCA § 49-6-2202(a)(3). Until and unless a formal waiver is granted under applicable State Board rules, districts may use these materials as supplemental materials only — not as the primary 180-day instructional vehicle.

What has been built

The materials consist of three interconnected layers:

  1. An online textbook site (35 HTML files, ~900KB compressed) organized into front matter, ten unit chapters, assessments, teacher resources, standards documentation, and compliance documentation.
  1. Three standalone supplemental packets designed for printing and independent use:

- Packet A — Skills & Practice (student-facing): SOAPS, HIPP, historical thinking skills practice, sourcing and corroboration exercises.

- Packet B — Inquiry & Writing (student-facing): All ten Open Inquiry Tasks with argument planning and product choice.

- Packet C — Teacher Scaffolds & Supports (teacher-facing only): Legal sidebar, AP sourcing support, Priority Teaching Guides, differentiation guidance.

  1. Internal compliance and adoption documentation: Schedule F self-assessment, waiver-readiness dossier, standards alignment matrix, EOC validation, publisher declaration, district adoption package.

Why this structure

The instructional design reflects Tennessee's Schedule F rubric expectations for high school social studies — specifically the requirements that materials embed disciplinary practices authentically, use essential questions that promote genuine inquiry, provide structured support for evidence-based writing, and address accessibility for all learners including students with disabilities and English Language Learners. The supplemental packet structure allows teachers to use any component independently, which matters for both adoption decisions and classroom flexibility.

The three instructional frameworks (SOAPS, HIPP, historical thinking skill labels) are sequenced to mirror how historians actually encounter and use sources: identification first (SOAPS), then argumentative analysis (HIPP), then disciplinary categorization (skill labels). This sequence is grounded in Samuel Wineburg's research on how expert historians read primary sources and in Adrienne Reisman's randomized controlled study of the "Reading Like a Historian" curriculum.

2.2 Front Matter & Skills

What it contains: The HIPP Analysis reference page (hipp.html) and the Research Basis document (methodology.html).

What it does: The HIPP page is the student-facing disciplinary skills reference for the entire course. It introduces SOAPS as the first-encounter tool and HIPP as the argumentative analysis tool, explains how they connect, and provides the historical thinking skill definitions used throughout all ten units. It is the anchor page that students should have open whenever they are working with a primary source.

Who uses it: Both students and teachers. The teacher note box at the bottom of hipp.html is teacher-facing only and covers AP alignment language and placement suggestions.

Key design decision: SOAPS and HIPP are presented as a sequence (SOAPS first, HIPP second) rather than as alternatives. This is intentional — SOAPS reduces cognitive load during first encounter; HIPP builds the argumentative connection required for scoring credit in evidence-based writing.

3. The Three Instructional Frameworks

This section describes the three analytical frameworks embedded throughout the course and explains how they work together.

3.1 SOAPS — Primary Source Identification

What it is: A five-element framework for initial primary source encounter: Speaker, Occasion, Audience, Purpose, Subject.

When to use it: Every time a student first encounters a primary source, before analysis begins. SOAPS reduces cognitive load by making the source's identity explicit before the student engages with its content. It is the "who, when, for whom, why, what" check.

Where it lives:

  • - Reference table: hipp.html Section 1
  • - Full practice tasks (3 public domain sources): packet-a-skills.html Section 1
  • - Combined SOAPS+HIPP table: packet-c-teacher.html Section 3, ell-support.html Part 3
  • - Embedded in every primary source spotlight in units 1–10

Research basis: Wineburg (2001) Historical Thinking and Other Unnatural Acts; Reisman (2012) randomized controlled study of document-based instruction (d = 0.52 on historical thinking; d = 0.37 on reading comprehension).

3.2 HIPP — Argumentative Document Analysis

What it is: A four-element framework for connecting source features to historical arguments: Historical Context, Intended Audience, Purpose, Point of View.

When to use it: After SOAPS — when building an argument using a source. HIPP asks not what the source is but why its features matter for the specific claim being made. The key move is the argumentative connection: "Because [HIPP feature], this source is [more/less useful] for arguing [specific claim] because ___."

Where it lives:

  • - Full reference + sentence stems + DBQ guidance: hipp.html
  • - HIPP + SOAPS comparison: hipp.html Section "HIPP + SOAPS"
  • - Practice tasks (3 items): packet-a-skills.html Section 2
  • - Combined SOAPS+HIPP form: ell-support.html Part 3
  • - Teacher modeling examples: packet-c-teacher.html Section 2
  • - Integration guide: packet-c-teacher.html Section 3
  • - data-historical-skill attribute on relevant HTML elements

Research basis: College Board APUSH CED (2023) — sourcing skill definition; Monte-Sano (2008) — empirical study documenting the identification/connection gap that HIPP closes.

Critical distinction: SOAPS identification alone does not earn sourcing credit in evidence-based writing. HIPP is the tool that makes the argumentative connection explicit. This distinction is modeled in the teacher AP sourcing support box (packet-c-teacher.html Section 2) with three versions of the same sentence (no credit → weak → full).

3.3 Historical Thinking Skill Labels

What they are: Five explicit disciplinary skill labels used throughout all unit activities:

LabelSkillCore Question
`[CAUSATION]`Identify and evaluate multiple causes of historical eventsWhy did this happen?
`[COMPARISON]`Analyze similarities and differences across events, policies, or groupsHow are these alike and different? What does that reveal?
`[CCOT]`Track what transformed and what persisted across a time periodWhat changed? What stayed the same? Why?
`[CHRONOLOGY]`Sequence events and explain how timing shaped outcomesIn what order? How did sequence matter?
`[CONTEXTUALIZATION]`Situate an event or source within its broader historical momentWhat was happening when this occurred? How did context shape it?

When to use them: Skill labels appear in ALL CAPS brackets at the end of activity titles (e.g., "Document Analysis — Hoover and the Depression [CAUSATION]"). The first time a label appears in each unit, a one-sentence student-facing definition appears in a shaded box below the activity header.

Where they live:

  • - Definitions: hipp.html Section "Historical Thinking Skills" (student-facing)
  • - Practice tasks (one per skill): packet-a-skills.html Section 3
  • - Activity title labels: every unit HTML file
  • - data-historical-skill attribute on tagged HTML elements
  • - Skill column: standards-matrix.html

Research basis: NCSS C3 Framework (2013) — the authoritative framework Tennessee SSPs are based on; Wineburg, Martin & Monte-Sano (2013) — classroom-validated curriculum using these same labels.

4.2 Packet B — Inquiry & Writing (Student)

File: packet-b-inquiry.html

Intended use: Assign the relevant unit task when students reach that unit; or assign multiple tasks as a semester research project with product choice.

Structure for each of the ten tasks:

  1. Unit and essential question header
  2. Open historical question (no single right answer; defensible positions expected)
  3. Curated source menu (5–6 sources per unit; all from materials or publicly verified)
  4. Step 1: Source selection table (source chosen / what it can prove / key HIPP feature)
  5. Step 2: Thesis draft
  6. Step 3: Product choice (short essay / podcast script / mini-documentary storyboard / public testimony speech)
  7. Step 4: Draft space

Good Historical Argument Checklist (printed on the first page): six criteria including claim specificity, two-source minimum, cross-source reasoning, HIPP connection, skill label application, and counterargument acknowledgment.

6. Assessment Architecture

6.1 Three-tier structure

FORMATIVE (data-assessment-type="formative")
  → Bell ringers (learning-science.html, one per unit)
  → Spaced retrieval rounds (3 per unit, increasing intervals)
  → In-lesson checks for understanding (embedded in unit narrative sections)
  → Exit tickets (learning-science.html)

PRACTICE / UNIT ASSESSMENT (activities.html, Version B)
  → 4 multiple choice items (DOK 1–2, TCAP EOC format)
  → 2 short-answer items (DOK 2–3)
  → 1 constructed response (1–2 paragraphs, with synthesis trigger)

SUMMATIVE (data-assessment-type="summative")
  → Version C DBQ: 4 documents, full sourcing + contextualization + argumentation
  → Version B extended essay: 10-point rubric
  → Version A CER with scaffold: 9-point rubric, graphic organizer pre-filled

6.2 Question bank structure

The question-bank.html file contains 447 items across US.01–US.44. Each item has:

  • - Standard code
  • - DOK level (1–3)
  • - Assessment type tag (formative/summative)
  • - Answer key with distractor analysis
  • - EOC frequency reference (5-year data)

Gap: US.45–US.95 assessment items do not yet exist. This is a Phase 1 priority item — the Gateway criterion G1 requires standards mapping for all US. standard codes before formal Commission submission.

6.3 Synthesis requirement

All DBQ and multi-source summative tasks include a synthesis trigger sentence appended to the main prompt. The trigger requires:

  1. Evidence from at least two different sources
  2. An explicit explanation of how the sources corroborate, contradict, or complicate each other

Unit-specific synthesis triggers exist for all ten units (see TroopToTeacher_CopyEdits_Package.html, Section 3). Generic options are available for any unit.

8.3 The sourcing skill progression across the year

The SOAPS → HIPP → Skill Label sequence maps to a year-long progression:

UNITS 1–3 (Establish the tools)
  SOAPS: Introduce, model, and practice with every primary source
  HIPP: Introduce Historical Context and Intended Audience only
  Skill Labels: CAUSATION and CHRONOLOGY (most concrete skills first)

UNITS 4–7 (Build the analysis)
  SOAPS: Fluency — students complete independently, teacher circulates
  HIPP: Add Purpose and Point of View; require one argumentative connection sentence
  Skill Labels: Add COMPARISON, CCOT, CONTEXTUALIZATION

UNITS 8–10 (Transfer and synthesis)
  SOAPS: Abbreviated (5 minutes); treat as routine, not a lesson
  HIPP: Full four-element analysis with argumentative connection required
  Skill Labels: All five; cross-unit synthesis tasks expected
