- **Factual caveats:**
  - The series is nonwhite workers ("nonwhites, of whom about 96 percent are Negroes") in firms reporting to the War Manpower Commission, not all U.S. employment.
  - Early 1942 value is rendered as an OPEN MARKER at 3.0% with an annotation "<3% (early 1942), open marker = upper bound, not exact" — conservatively, not as a false precise 3.0% data point. Subsequent points (Sep 1942 = 4.6%, Jan 1943 = 6.4%, Jan 1944 = 7.2%, Nov 1944 = 8.3%) are exact as published.
  - The NARA migration callout (~6,000,000 total Black migrants, 1910s-1970s) is a SEPARATE multi-decade population-movement figure. It is NOT an annual series and must not be implied as directly linked, year-by-year, to the FEPC employment-share series.
- **Alt text:** Line chart showing the nonwhite share of workers in War Manpower Commission-reporting firms rising from under 3% in early 1942 (shown as an open/uncertain marker) to 8.3% in November 1944, with a separate callout box noting the National Archives' approximately six-million-person Great Migration estimate for 1910s-1970s.
