How to Use This Packet

  • Part 1 — Sentence frames: Use these frames to start any historical analysis sentence. Select the frame that matches your skill (Causation, Comparison, etc.) and fill in the blanks with specific historical content. Practice writing at least one complete sentence after each set.
  • Part 2 — Vocabulary scaffold: Review the key analytical terms before every writing task. Spanish translations are a bridge — practice using the English terms in your own sentences.
  • Parts 3 & 4 — Organizers and word wall: Use the graphic organizers with any unit throughout the year. The word wall gives student-friendly definitions for essential historical terms organized by era.

Part 1 — Universal Sentence Frames for Historical Analysis

These frames work with any unit and any historical thinking skill. Choose the frame that matches your task. Replace the brackets [ ] with specific historical content. Write at least one complete practice sentence after each set.

General Frames — Starting Any Response

Use when you need to start a sentence or connect two ideas.

In [time period], [historical group] faced _____ because _____.According to [source], _____ happened because _____.This source was created by _____, who wanted _____.One important event during this period was _____, which caused _____.The evidence shows that _____ because _____.I agree / disagree with this argument because _____.This source supports my argument because _____.One limitation of this source is _____ because _____.

CCOT Frames — Continuity and Change Over Time [CCOT]

Use when explaining what changed, what stayed the same, or how quickly change happened over a period.

Before [date], _____. After [date], _____ had changed because _____.By [date], _____ had changed significantly because _____.Despite the changes, one thing that stayed the same was _____ because _____.The most significant change between _____ and _____ was _____.

Use when connecting a source or event to the broader historical moment surrounding it.

Use when identifying who created a source, why, and for whom.

The speaker of this source is _____, who is significant because _____.This source was created during [occasion], when _____ was happening.The audience for this source was _____, which explains why the author _____.The purpose of this source was to _____, which means it is most useful as evidence of _____.This source is about _____, which connects to the essential question by _____.

Part 2 — Academic Vocabulary Scaffold

These are the key analytical terms used in U.S. History writing tasks. Study the definition and the model sentence. Then write your own sentences in the practice section below the table.

Term (Término)What it meansModel sentence — study this pattern
Analyze
Analizar		 Examine something closely and explain how its parts work together. Analyze how the New Deal changed the relationship between the government and citizens.
Argue
Argumentar		 Make a claim and support it with evidence. Argue whether Reconstruction was a success or failure for African Americans.
Evaluate
Evaluar		 Judge how important, successful, or valid something is, using evidence. Evaluate the extent to which the Civil Rights Act achieved its goals.
Compare
Comparar		 Identify both similarities and differences between two things. Compare Reagan's economic policies to FDR's New Deal programs.
Explain
Explicar		 Describe clearly how or why something happened. Explain two causes of the Great Depression.
Evidence
Evidencia		 Specific facts, examples, or data that support a claim. Use evidence from the primary source to support your claim.
Claim
Afirmación / Tesis		 Your main argument — a direct answer to the historical question. Write a claim that directly answers the essential question.
Primary source
Fuente primaria		 A document, image, or artifact created during the historical period being studied. Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address is a primary source that reveals his goals for Reconstruction.
Secondary source
Fuente secundaria		 A work created later by a historian analyzing the past. A history textbook is a secondary source because it interprets and summarizes primary sources.
Corroborate
Corroborar		 Confirm or strengthen an argument using two or more independent sources. Corroborate your argument about the causes of WWI using both the data chart and the primary source.
Contextualize
Contextualizar		 Connect a source or event to the broader historical situation surrounding it. Contextualize the Emancipation Proclamation within the military and political pressures of the Civil War.
Point of view
Punto de vista		 The perspective shaped by a person's identity, experiences, and interests. The factory owner and the factory worker had very different points of view on the 1894 Pullman Strike.
Practice — Choose three terms from the table. Write your own sentence using each one: 1. 2. 3.

Part 4 — Era-by-Era Word Wall

Organized by the 10 units of the course. Each term is followed by a student-friendly definition and the Spanish translation.

Reconstruction / Reconstrucción
The period 1865–1877 when the U.S. tried to reunite after the Civil War and define rights for freed people.
Freedmen / Libertos
African Americans freed from slavery after the Civil War.
Jim Crow laws / Leyes Jim Crow
State laws enforcing racial segregation in the South after 1877.
Sharecropping / Aparcería
A system keeping many freed people in poverty by working land they didn't own for a share of the crops.
Industrialization / Industrialización
The shift from farm-based to factory-based economy powered by railroads and machines.
Labor union / Sindicato
An organization of workers that bargains for better pay and conditions.
Monopoly / Monopolio
One company controlling an entire industry, eliminating competition.
Immigration / Inmigración
The movement of people from other countries to settle in the U.S., especially 1880–1920.
Suffrage / Sufragio
The right to vote.
Progressivism / Progresismo
A reform movement seeking to reduce inequality through government regulation.
Muckraker / Periodista de denuncia
A journalist who exposed corruption and injustice in American society.
Amendment / Enmienda
A change or addition to the U.S. Constitution.
Neutrality / Neutralidad
Not taking sides in a war or conflict.
Propaganda / Propaganda
Information designed to influence public opinion, often one-sided.
Isolationism / Aislacionismo
The belief that the U.S. should stay out of foreign conflicts and alliances.
Migration / Migración
The movement of large groups of people from one region to another — e.g., the Great Migration of African Americans to Northern cities.
Depression / Depresión económica
A severe economic crisis with high unemployment, falling prices, and bank failures.
Unemployment / Desempleo
The condition of being unable to find work despite wanting to.
New Deal / Nuevo Trato
FDR's programs designed to provide relief, recovery, and reform during the Great Depression.
Federal government / Gobierno federal
The national government of the United States, as opposed to state governments.
Democracy / Democracia
A system of government in which citizens elect representatives and have protected rights.
Fascism / Fascismo
An extreme nationalist, authoritarian political system; the ideology of Nazi Germany and Mussolini's Italy.
Internment / Internamiento
The forced detention of a group of people — e.g., Japanese Americans during WWII.
Segregation / Segregación
The forced separation of racial groups in public spaces, schools, and the military.
Civil rights / Derechos civiles
The rights of citizens to political and social freedom and equality.
Nonviolent protest / Protesta no violenta
A strategy of peaceful civil disobedience to challenge unjust laws.
Containment / Contención
The U.S. foreign policy of preventing the spread of communism.
Legislation / Legislación
Laws passed by Congress.
Escalation / Escalada
A gradual increase in military involvement or conflict.
Credibility gap / Brecha de credibilidad
The growing gap between what the government said about Vietnam and what the public learned was actually happening.
Impeachment / Juicio político
The process by which Congress can formally charge a president with misconduct.
Inflation / Inflación
A general rise in prices, reducing the purchasing power of money.
Deregulation / Desregulación
Reducing government rules and oversight of businesses.
Tax cut / Reducción de impuestos
A reduction in the taxes that individuals or businesses pay to the government.
Cold War / Guerra Fría
The tense standoff between the U.S. and Soviet Union from 1947 to 1991.
Globalization / Globalización
The increasing economic and cultural connections between countries worldwide.
Terrorism / Terrorismo
The use of violence against civilians to create fear and achieve political goals.
Civil liberties / Libertades civiles
Rights and freedoms protected by law, such as freedom of speech and protection from unreasonable search.
Polarization / Polarización
The increasing division between political groups, making compromise more difficult.
Surveillance / Vigilancia
Monitoring or watching people's activities, often by the government.
