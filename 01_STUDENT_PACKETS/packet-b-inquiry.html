What is an Open Inquiry Task? Each task presents a genuine historical question — one historians answer differently depending on which evidence they examine and how they weigh it. Your job is not to find the "right answer." Your job is to construct the most defensible argument you can from the available sources.

Good Historical Argument Checklist

Before submitting any response from this packet, use this checklist:

Specific claim: My thesis directly answers the historical question — not just "there are many factors."
Evidence from at least two sources: I cite specific evidence from at least two of the sources listed in the task.
Cross-source reasoning: I explain how my sources corroborate or complicate each other — not just summarize each one separately.
At least one HIPP connection: For at least one source, I explain how its Historical Context, Audience, Purpose, or Point of View strengthens my argument.
Historical thinking skill applied: I have explicitly used the skill labeled on this task (CAUSATION, COMPARISON, CCOT, or CONTEXTUALIZATION) — not just implied it.
Counterargument acknowledged: I identify at least one reasonable challenge to my argument and explain why my evidence is stronger.
