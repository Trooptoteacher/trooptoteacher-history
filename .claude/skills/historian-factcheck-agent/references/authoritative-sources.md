# Authoritative Source Canon — Historian Factcheck Agent

This is the registry of sources you are permitted to treat as verifying evidence. Any claim verified using a source OFF this canon is not truly verified. If you cannot find a verifying source on this canon, the claim is CANNOT VERIFY — never upgrade a non-canonical source to canonical status mid-run.

---

## Tier 1 — Primary Statutes, Cases, and Treaties

### Federal statutes
- **GovInfo** — https://www.govinfo.gov — U.S. Statutes at Large, Public Laws, Compilations (e.g., `COMPS-3055` for the Sherman Antitrust Act). THE canonical source for statute volume, page, enactment date.
- **Library of Congress THOMAS / Congress.gov** — https://www.congress.gov — for current and historical federal legislation
- **National Archives (NARA)** — https://www.archives.gov — for original enrolled acts and signed documents
- **Teaching Legal History, Univ. of Nebraska–Lincoln** — for hard-to-find 19th-century statutes with transcriptions

### Federal court cases
- **U.S. Reports (official)** — https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes.aspx — THE canonical source for Supreme Court opinions with volume/page/year
- **Justia (U.S. Supreme Court Center)** — https://supreme.justia.com — reliable transcriptions and metadata
- **Oyez (Cornell/Chicago-Kent)** — https://www.oyez.org — case summaries, oral arguments, audio, majority/dissent authorship
- **Cornell Law School LII** — https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/ — full opinion texts with reliable citations
- **Federal Reporter, Federal Supplement, state supreme court reporters** — for non-SCOTUS federal and state cases

### Treaties
- **National Archives Treaty Records** — https://www.archives.gov/research/treaties — federal treaties with Native nations, foreign treaties
- **Charles J. Kappler, *Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties*** — public-domain compilation of U.S.–Tribal treaties through 1904 (available via Oklahoma State University Library)

### State constitutions and statutes
- **State secretary of state websites and state archives** — for state constitutional provisions (e.g., Mississippi Constitution of 1890, Art. XII)
- **HeinOnline** — if the operator has institutional access, otherwise use state archive sources

## Tier 2 — Federal Agency Data

### U.S. Census Bureau
- **Historical Census Statistics** — https://www.census.gov/library/publications/time-series.html
- **Decennial Census tables (1790–2020)** — https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial-census/decade.html
- **Historical Statistics of the United States** — millennial edition (Cambridge U. Press + Census) for pre-1950 data
- **1900 Census published reports** — for child labor figures, immigration figures, occupational breakdowns
- For 19th-century occupational and demographic claims, the published Census reports of the decade in question are canonical.

### Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
- **BLS historical series** — https://www.bls.gov/data/
- For labor disputes, wage data, employment statistics post-1884 (BLS founded 1884)

### Government Accountability Office (GAO) — for federal program audits and data

### Department of the Interior
- **Bureau of Indian Affairs historical records** — https://www.bia.gov
- **Indian Claims Commission records** — via National Archives
- For allotment records, reservation lands, treaty administration

### National Park Service
- **NPS historical records** — https://www.nps.gov
- For site-specific events (Promontory Summit, Golden Spike, Little Bighorn, Homestead National Monument, etc.)

### Bureau of Land Management (BLM)
- For land grant records, homestead patents, railroad land grant administration
- **GLO Records** — https://glorecords.blm.gov — for original land patents

### Department of State / Office of the Historian
- **https://history.state.gov** — for diplomatic history, foreign-relations primary documents

## Tier 3 — Institutional Repositories and Primary-Source Archives

### Library of Congress (LOC)
- **Chronicling America** — https://chroniclingamerica.loc.gov — historical newspaper archive (for event dates, contemporary accounts)
- **LOC Digital Collections** — https://www.loc.gov/collections/ — photographs, manuscripts, maps, oral histories
- **LOC Research Guides** — for topical starting points (use as leads, not as primary verification)

### National Archives (NARA) — beyond statutes/treaties
- **NARA Catalog** — https://catalog.archives.gov
- For federal records, photographs, military service records, slave schedules, allotment records, boarding-school records

### Smithsonian Institution
- **National Museum of American History, National Museum of the American Indian, National Museum of African American History and Culture**
- For artifact-based claims, exhibit documentation, oral histories

### State historical societies
- **Tennessee State Library and Archives** (critical for TN-specific claims)
- **Other state historical societies** — for state-specific events, biographies, documents

### Specialized repositories
- **National Native American Boarding School Healing Coalition** — https://boardingschoolhealing.org — canonical for boarding school enrollment, school lists, survivor testimony
- **Chinese Historical Society of America** — for Chinese immigration, labor, community records
- **Sons & Daughters of the U.S. Middle Passage, 1619 Project primary-source appendix** — as leads to primary sources, not as citations themselves
- **Yale's Gilder Lehrman Center / Gilder Lehrman Institute** — https://www.gilderlehrman.org — curated primary source collections (use cited documents, not the platform commentary)
- **Digital Public Library of America (DPLA)** — https://dp.la — aggregator of verified archives; always cite the underlying institution, not "DPLA"
- **American Yawp Reader** — https://www.americanyawp.com/reader/ — open-access primary source compilation (use cited documents, not commentary)

### University archives and special collections
- Major research-university archives (Yale, Harvard, Berkeley, UCLA, Stanford, Princeton, Columbia, Chicago, Michigan, Texas, UNC, Duke, etc.)
- **University of Nebraska-Lincoln's Teaching Legal History** — for 19th-century legal documents
- **Columbia University DLC (Digital Library Collection)** — for periodical archives including *North American Review*

### Periodicals (when original print run is cited)
- **JSTOR** — for verified digital reproductions of original periodical articles; cite the original publication, not JSTOR URL
- Original journal archives (e.g., *North American Review*, *Harper's Weekly*, *Atlantic Monthly*) via university digital collections

## Tier 4 — Tribal Records, Oral Histories, Community Archives

These are canonical for their subject matter. Do not treat federal records as a superior source on tribal history.

- **Tribal archives and cultural preservation offices** of individual Native nations
- **Doctrine of Discovery Project** / **Truth and Healing Commissions** documentation
- **Oral history archives** administered by recognized institutions (e.g., Smithsonian's Recovering Voices, UCLA's Oral History Center, Shoah Foundation)

---

## NOT Acceptable as Verifying Sources

The following may be used as **leads** to find canonical sources, but NEVER as the verifying citation in a report:

- **Wikipedia article text** — use its references to find the canonical source, then cite that source directly
- **Quora, Reddit, Stack Exchange** — not verifying sources, ever
- **Blog posts without sourced documentation** — not verifying sources
- **AI-generated summaries or assistants (other than this session's own tools when used to LOOK UP a canonical source)** — an AI saying "yes that's correct" is not verification
- **Textbook claims without a primary-source footnote** — the textbook is a secondary source; find its underlying source
- **Museum exhibit didactic text** without underlying documentation — exhibit signage is curatorial, not canonical
- **YouTube videos, podcasts, TED talks** — even when by reputable historians; cite their published work instead
- **News opinion columns** — op-eds are not canonical even from respected papers
- **Partisan advocacy organizations** (from any part of the political spectrum) — their data may be real but cite the underlying source, not the advocacy summary
- **Unpublished dissertations** — unless the specific claim is only documented there and the dissertation is available via ProQuest or a university repository; cite with full diss. citation and acknowledge limitation

## Triangulation Rule

For any numerical claim (population, acreage, casualties, dollars, enrollment, etc.):

1. **Prefer federal agency data** if the figure is within a federal mandate's scope (e.g., Census for population, BLS for labor, Interior for land and Native affairs)
2. **Accept institutional-repository data** if the figure is outside federal scope (e.g., boarding school enrollment = Boarding School Healing Coalition; tribal membership = tribal archives)
3. **If only secondary-source historians agree on a figure**, flag as NEEDS CITATION with a recommendation to cite the leading historian's work (e.g., Lew-Williams on Chinese expulsion figures, Blackhawk on Native history figures, Hämäläinen on Plains populations)
4. **If no authoritative source gives the specific number cited**, mark CANNOT VERIFY and recommend softening to a documented range

## Common 19th–20th Century Citation Patterns

### Statute citations (U.S.)
Format: `[Act Name], [Vol] Stat. [Page] ([Month Day, Year]).`
Example: `Homestead Act, 12 Stat. 392 (May 20, 1862).`
Verify via GovInfo or National Archives.

### Case citations (U.S. Supreme Court)
Format: `[*Case Name*], [Vol] U.S. [Page] ([Year]).`
Example: `*Plessy v. Ferguson*, 163 U.S. 537 (1896).`
Verify via Justia, Oyez, or Cornell LII.

### Federal regulations
Format: `[Vol] Fed. Reg. [Page] ([Date]).`
Verify via the *Federal Register* (federalregister.gov).

### Executive orders
Format: `Executive Order [Number], [Vol] Fed. Reg. [Page] ([Date]).`
Verify via NARA or the Federal Register.

### Treaty citations
Format: `Treaty of [Name], [Date], [Stat. or TS citation].`
Verify via NARA or Kappler's compilation.

### State constitutions
Format: `[State] Constitution of [Year], Art. [Roman numeral], § [number].`
Verify via state archive.

### Historical newspaper citations
Format: `[Article title], *[Newspaper]*, [Date], p. [page].`
Verify via Chronicling America or newspaper's archive.
