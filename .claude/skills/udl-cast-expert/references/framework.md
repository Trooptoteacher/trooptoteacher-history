# UDL 3.0 Framework — Complete Reference

Source: [udlguidelines.cast.org](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) (CAST, 2024-07-30)

## Overall Goal

Learner agency that is **purposeful & reflective, resourceful & authentic, strategic & action-oriented**.

CAST tagline: **Until learning has no limits.**

CAST's operating premise: The barriers to learning are in the design of the environment, not in the learner. UDL is a proactive framework for designing curriculum, materials, and environments that anticipate learner variability.

---

## Principle 1 — Design Multiple Means of Engagement

*Brain network:* Affective networks
*Question:* The **WHY** of learning

**Goal statement (CAST exact):** Affect represents a crucial element to the learning process, and learners differ markedly in what sparks their motivation and enthusiasm for learning. Learners must be able to bring their authentic selves to the learning environment and find connections to what matters most in their lives. The UDL framework emphasizes the idea of learner variability, and learners' multiple and intersecting identities are an essential layer of fully recognizing the notion of learner variability. While we emphasize dimensions of identity as a part of variability in this section on Multiple Means of Engagement, the essential role identity plays in learning is woven across all three UDL principles.

### Guideline 7 — Welcoming Interests & Identities

- **7.1 Optimize choice and autonomy** — Provide learners with real, meaningful choices in how they engage with content, materials, and tasks. Not "choose your color of paper" — genuine choices in topic, sequence, medium, or approach.
- **7.2 Optimize relevance, value, and authenticity** — Design tasks that connect to learners' lives, identities, futures. Authentic audience, authentic purpose, authentic product.
- **7.3 Nurture joy and play** — Playful design, humor, curiosity-driven inquiry are legitimate UDL considerations. Not decoration — foundational.
- **7.4 Address biases, threats, and distractions** — Actively surface and remove bias in prompts, examples, imagery. Reduce threat (stereotype threat, evaluative anxiety) through design.

### Guideline 8 — Sustaining Effort & Persistence

- **8.1 Clarify the meaning and purpose of goals** — Learners know WHY they're learning this, not just WHAT.
- **8.2 Optimize challenge and support** — The zone of proximal development is a design constraint, not a teacher's judgment call. Multiple entry points at different challenge levels.
- **8.3 Foster collaboration, interdependence, and collective learning** — Design tasks that require or reward mutual reliance, not just individual mastery.
- **8.4 Foster belonging and community** — Every learner has a role in the classroom community by design.
- **8.5 Offer action-oriented feedback** — Feedback is specific, actionable, forward-looking — not grades.

### Guideline 9 — Emotional Capacity

*(Replaced 2.2 "Self-Regulation." 3.0 recognizes emotion as foundational, not just something to manage.)*

- **9.1 Recognize expectations, beliefs, and motivations** — Learners bring beliefs about their own competence; design should surface and reshape these.
- **9.2 Develop awareness of self and others** — Metacognition and empathy as design outcomes.
- **9.3 Promote individual and collective reflection** — Reflection built into the artifact, not tacked on.
- **9.4 Cultivate empathy and restorative practices** — Explicit design for restorative rather than punitive responses to conflict, error, or struggle.

## Principle 2 — Design Multiple Means of Representation

*Brain network:* Recognition networks
*Question:* The **WHAT** of learning

**Goal statement (CAST exact):** Learners differ in the ways they perceive and make meaning of information. For example, those with sensory disabilities (e.g., blindness or deafness), learning disabilities (e.g., dyslexia), and those representing diverse or non-dominant cultures and/or languages all approach content differently. And these differing approaches must be honored and valued. Equally important is the consideration of how people, cultures, individual and collective identities, perspectives, and ways of knowing are represented within the content. Learning, and transfer of learning, occurs when multiple representations and perspectives are used.

### Guideline 1 — Perception

*Statement:* Interact with flexible content that offers multiple modalities and perspectives.

- **1.1 Support opportunities to customize the display of information** — Font, contrast, spacing, color, size are learner-adjustable, not fixed.
- **1.2 Support multiple ways to perceive information** — Same content available via text, audio, image, video, tactile.
- **1.3 Represent a diversity of perspectives and identities in authentic ways** — Content itself represents multiple identities/perspectives; not just one dominant lens with token exceptions.

### Guideline 2 — Language & Symbols

*Statement:* Communicate through languages that create a shared understanding.

- **2.1 Clarify vocabulary, symbols, and language structures** — Vocab pre-teach, symbol glossaries, sentence-structure supports.
- **2.2 Support decoding of text, mathematical notation, and symbols** — Decoding scaffolds (audio, phonetic, structural).
- **2.3 Cultivate understanding and respect across languages and dialects** — Explicit valuation of home languages, dialects, code-switching as legitimate.
- **2.4 Address biases in the use of language and symbols** — Audit content for language that centers dominant cultures or excludes.
- **2.5 Illustrate through multiple media** — Ideas represented in more than one medium.

### Guideline 3 — Building Knowledge

*Statement:* Construct meaning and generate new understandings.

- **3.1 Connect prior knowledge to new learning** — Explicit design that surfaces and builds on what learners already know.
- **3.2 Highlight and explore patterns, critical features, big ideas, and relationships** — The organizing structure of the content is visible.
- **3.3 Cultivate multiple ways of knowing and making meaning** — Indigenous, cultural, experiential, disciplinary ways of knowing are treated as valid.
- **3.4 Maximize transfer and generalization** — Design that surfaces connections across contexts and prompts transfer.

## Principle 3 — Design Multiple Means of Action & Expression

*Brain network:* Strategic networks
*Question:* The **HOW** of learning

**Goal statement (CAST exact):** Learners differ in the ways they navigate a learning environment, approach the learning process, and express what they know. Therefore, it is essential to design for and honor these varying forms of action and expression. For example, all individuals, including those with disabilities, approach learning tasks very differently. Depending on the context, some may prefer to express themselves in written text but not speech, and vice versa. It should also be recognized that action and expression require a great deal of strategy, practice, and organization.

### Guideline 4 — Interaction

- **4.1 Vary and honor the methods for response, navigation, and movement** — Physical, digital, oral, gestural, tactile responses accepted.
- **4.2 Optimize access to accessible materials and assistive and accessible technologies and tools** — Compatibility with AT (screen readers, switches, dictation) is a design requirement.

### Guideline 5 — Expression & Communication

- **5.1 Use multiple media for communication** — Writing, speaking, drawing, video, audio, code, physical models — all valid modes to express understanding.
- **5.2 Use multiple tools for construction, composition, and creativity** — Tools of expression are varied; no monopoly on "the way to show your work."
- **5.3 Build fluencies with graduated support for practice and performance** — Structured practice with fading scaffolds — not thrown in the deep end.
- **5.4 Address biases related to modes of expression and communication** — Written English is not the only legitimate mode; audit for cultural/linguistic bias in what's rewarded.

### Guideline 6 — Strategy Development

*(Replaced 2.2 "Executive Functions." 3.0 shifted from a neuroscience label to a learning-strategy label.)*

- **6.1 Set meaningful goals** — Goal-setting is a designed activity, not assumed.
- **6.2 Anticipate and plan for challenges** — Design surfaces predictable difficulty and gives strategies for it.
- **6.3 Organize information and resources** — Templates, note structures, digital organizers built into the artifact.
- **6.4 Enhance capacity for monitoring progress** — Progress-checking built in; learner self-assesses.
- **6.5 Challenge exclusionary practices** — **NEW in 3.0.** Actively identify and dismantle practices that exclude — tracking, punitive grading, single-mode assessment, cultural gatekeeping.
