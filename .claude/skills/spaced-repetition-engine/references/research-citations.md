## Retrieval Practice (Testing Effect)

### Roediger, H. L., & Butler, A. C. (2011)
- **Title**: The critical role of retrieval practice in long-term retention
- **Journal**: *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 15(1), 20–27
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.tics.2010.09.003
- **Effect Size**: *d* = 0.50–0.80
- **Key Finding**: Retrieval practice is more effective than restudying for long-term retention. Testing not only assesses learning but strengthens it. The act of retrieval modifies memory, making it more accessible in the future.
- **Design Implication**: All review must be active recall (MC, short answer) — never passive flashcard display.

### Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006)
- **Title**: Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention
- **Journal**: *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249–255
- **DOI**: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
- **Effect Size**: 80% superiority on 1-week delayed test
- **Key Finding**: Students who took practice tests remembered 80% more on a delayed test than students who spent the same time restudying. The benefit increased over time — testing was especially powerful for long-term retention vs. short-term.
- **Design Implication**: Spiral review questions should be low-stakes retrieval practice, not graded assessment.

## Spaced Repetition

### Ebbinghaus, H. (1885/1913)
- **Title**: *Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology*
- **Publisher**: Teachers College, Columbia University
- **URL**: https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Ebbinghaus/memory1.htm
- **Effect Size**: 150–200% savings in relearning time
- **Key Finding**: Memory decays exponentially: ~40% forgotten within 20 minutes, 56% within 1 hour, 77% within 6 days without review. Distributed study dramatically reduces repetitions needed for relearning.
- **Design Implication**: First review within 24 hours of initial learning. Expanding intervals thereafter.

### Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006)
- **Title**: Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis
- **Journal**: *Psychological Bulletin*, 132(3), 354–380
- **DOI**: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354
- **Effect Size**: *d* = 0.40–0.90 (median ~0.60)
- **Key Finding**: Meta-analysis of 254 studies (n > 14,000). Spacing benefit increases with longer retention intervals. For tests weeks or months out, longer inter-study gaps are optimal.
- **Design Implication**: Intervals must expand — the Leitner box schedule (1→3→7→14→30 days) approximates the optimal gap function.

### Pashler, H., Rohrer, D., Cepeda, N. J., & Carpenter, S. K. (2007)
- **Title**: Enhancing learning and retarding forgetting: Choices and consequences
- **Journal**: *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 14(2), 187–193
- **URL**: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED505659.pdf
- **Effect Size**: Up to 150% improvement under optimal spacing
- **Key Finding**: "Mountain ridge" relationship — an optimal gap exists depending on the intended retention interval. For a test 1 month out, ~1 week inter-study interval is near-optimal. Too short barely helps; too long allows forgetting before review.
- **Design Implication**: EOC Exam Alignment Mode should compress intervals as exam approaches. Standard mode uses expanding gaps calibrated to semester-length retention.

## Interleaving

### Rohrer, D., & Taylor, K. (2007)
- **Title**: The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning
- **Journal**: *Instructional Science*, 35(6), 481–498
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8
- **Effect Size**: 25 percentage-point advantage (*d* ≈ 0.79) at 1-week delay
- **Key Finding**: Interleaved practice forces students to identify which strategy to apply, not just how to apply a known strategy. This produces superior transfer to exams.
- **Design Implication**: Never serve 3 questions from the same unit. Mix eras, topics, and question types.

### Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008)
- **Title**: Learning concepts and categories: Is spacing the "enemy of induction"?
- **Journal**: *Psychological Science*, 19(6), 585–592
- **Effect Size**: *d* ≈ 0.70
- **Key Finding**: Interleaving improves category discrimination — learners better recognize patterns when examples are mixed. Most students incorrectly believe blocked practice is more effective (metacognitive illusion).
- **Design Implication**: Explicitly tell students that the review will feel harder but produces better learning. Mix historical periods in each session.

## Formative Feedback

### Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007)
- **Title**: The power of feedback
- **Journal**: *Review of Educational Research*, 77(1), 81–112
- **DOI**: 10.3102/003465430298487
- **Effect Size**: *d* = 0.73 overall; process-level feedback produces largest gains
- **Key Finding**: Feedback must address three questions: Where am I going? How am I going? Where to next? Process-level feedback (strategies, reasoning) > task-level (right/wrong) > self-level (praise).
- **Design Implication**: Every spiral review response must include elaborated feedback explaining the historical reasoning, not just correctness.

### Shute, V. J. (2008)
- **Title**: Focus on formative feedback
- **Journal**: *Review of Educational Research*, 78(1), 153–189
- **DOI**: 10.3102/0034654307313795
- **Effect Size**: *d* = 0.40–0.80+
- **Key Finding**: Elaborated feedback (correct answer + explanation) consistently outperforms verification-only. Immediate feedback aids factual recall. Adaptive systems that respond to individual learners outperform one-size-fits-all.
- **Design Implication**: Show correct answer + why immediately after selection. Per-distractor misconception feedback where available.

## Instructional Principles

### Rosenshine, B. (2012)
- **Title**: Principles of instruction: Research-based strategies that all teachers should know
- **Journal**: *American Educator*, 36(1), 12–39
- **URL**: https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/Rosenshine.pdf
- **Effect Size**: Direct instruction framework *d* = 0.59 (Hattie); individual principles up to *d* = 0.70
- **Key Finding**: Principle 1 (Daily Review) and Principle 10 (Weekly/Monthly Review) directly mandate spiral review as a core instructional structure. Effective teachers begin every lesson with prior-content review.
- **Design Implication**: Spiral review is not optional — it is a research-mandated daily instructional routine.

### Bjork, R. A. (1994)
- **Title**: Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings
- **Source**: In J. Metcalfe & A. Shimamura (Eds.), *Metacognition: Knowing about knowing* (pp. 185–205). MIT Press
- **Key Finding**: "Desirable difficulties" — conditions that make learning harder in the short term (spacing, interleaving, testing) produce better long-term retention. Target ~80% success rate.
- **Design Implication**: Track per-student accuracy. If consistently >90%, increase difficulty. If <60%, ease back.

## Algorithm Foundation

### SM-2 Algorithm (Wozniak, 1990)
- **Title**: Optimization of repetition spacing in the practice of learning
- **Source**: Master's thesis, University of Technology, Poznań
- **URL**: https://www.supermemo.com/en/archives1990-2015/english/ol/sm2
- **Key Finding**: The SuperMemo 2 algorithm uses an ease factor (starting at 2.5) that adjusts based on response quality. Intervals expand multiplicatively. The algorithm has been validated across millions of users over 30+ years.
- **Design Implication**: Use ease factor to personalize interval expansion per student per standard. Students who struggle get shorter intervals; students who master quickly get longer ones.

### Leitner System (Leitner, 1972)
- **Title**: *So lernt man lernen* (Learning to Learn)
- **Key Finding**: Simple box-based system where cards move forward on correct answer and back to Box 1 on incorrect. Easy to understand, easy to implement. Proven effective for decades in language learning and factual recall.
- **Design Implication**: Combine Leitner's simplicity (5 boxes, clear rules) with SM-2's adaptive ease factor for a system that is both transparent and personalized.
