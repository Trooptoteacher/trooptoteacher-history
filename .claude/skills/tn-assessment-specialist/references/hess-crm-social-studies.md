# Hess Cognitive Rigor Matrix — Social Studies

Source: [Hess CRM Social Studies (Corwin)](https://resources.corwin.com/sites/default/files/tool_4_1.pdf)
© Karin Hess (2009, updated 2017). Permission to reproduce when authorship is fully cited.

## Purpose

The Hess CRM crosses Bloom's Revised Taxonomy (cognitive process) with Webb's DOK (depth/complexity) to produce specific cell-level descriptors. Use this matrix to:

1. Assign the correct DOK level based on what the item actually demands — not just the verb
2. Verify that a DOK label matches the cognitive process required
3. Ensure that items labeled DOK 1 are genuinely recall-level, not mislabeled DOK 2 tasks

**Critical principle**: A rigorous DOK 1 item is not automatically less challenging than a weak DOK 3 item. DOK measures complexity of thinking, not difficulty of content.

## Matrix

### Remember × DOK 1
- Recall or locate key facts, dates, terms, details, events, or ideas explicit in texts

### Understand × DOK 1
(No specific descriptors — pure recall of understood concepts defaults to Remember × DOK 1)

### Understand × DOK 2
- Select appropriate words/terms when intended meaning is clearly evident
- Describe or explain who, what
- Define facts, details, terms, principles
- Locate or identify symbols that represent...
- Raise related questions for possible investigation
- Specify, explain, illustrate relationships; explain why (cause-effect)
- Provide and explain non-examples/examples
- Summarize results, concepts, main ideas, generalizations
- Make basic inferences or logical predictions (using data/text)
- Locate relevant information to support explicit-implicit central ideas

### Understand × DOK 3
- Explain, generalize, or connect ideas using supporting evidence (quote, example, text reference, data)
- Support inferences about explicit or implicit themes
- Describe how word choice, point of view, or bias may affect the reader's/viewer's interpretation
- Write multi-paragraph composition or essay for specific purpose, focus, voice, tone, and audience
- Explain how concepts or ideas specifically relate to other content domains or concepts (social, political, historical, cultural)
- Apply generalizations to new problem-based situations

### Understand × DOK 4
- Use multiple sources to elaborate on how concepts or ideas specifically draw from other content domains or differing concepts (e.g., research paper, arguments of policy)

### Apply × DOK 2
- Apply basic formats for documenting sources
- Apply/use reference materials and tools for gathering information (e.g., keyword searches)
- Use context to identify the meaning of words/phrases
- Interpret information using text features (diagrams, data tables, captions)
- Apply simple organizational structures (paragraph, outline)
- Investigate to determine how a historical, cultural, or political context may be the source of an underlying theme, central idea, or unresolved issue or crisis

### Apply × DOK 3
- Integrate or juxtapose multiple (historical, cultural) contexts drawn from source materials with intent to develop a complex or multimedia product and personal viewpoint

### Analyze × DOK 2
- Identify causes or effects
- Describe processes or tools used to research ideas, artifacts, or images reflecting history, culture, tradition
- Identify ways symbols and metaphors are used to represent universal ideas
- Identify specific information given in graphics (map, T-chart, diagram) or text features (heading, subheading, captions)
- Compare similarities or differences in processes, methods, styles due to influences of time period, politics, or culture

### Analyze × DOK 3
- Distinguish relevant–irrelevant information, fact/opinion; primary from secondary source
- Draw inferences about social, historical, cultural contexts portrayed in literature, arts, film, political cartoons, primary sources
- Explain/categorize events, evolution of topic across time periods
- Analyze information within data sets or a text (interrelationships among concepts, issues, problems)
- Analyze an author's viewpoint or potential bias (political cartoon)
- Use reasoning, planning, and evidence to support or refute inferences in policy or speech
- Use reasoning and evidence to generate criteria for making and supporting an argument of judgment (e.g., Was FDR a great president? Is this a fair law?)

### Analyze × DOK 4
- Analyze multiple sources of evidence across time periods, themes, issues
- Analyze diverse complex abstract perspectives
- Gather, analyze, and organize information from multiple sources
- Analyze discourse styles or bias in speeches, legal briefs, etc., across time or authors
- Compare and contrast conflicting judgments or policies (e.g., Supreme Court decisions)

### Evaluate × DOK 3
- Develop a logical argument for conjectures, citing evidence
- Verify reasonableness of results of others
- Critique conclusions drawn, evidence used, credibility of sources
- Evaluate relevancy, accuracy, completeness of information using multiple sources
- Apply understanding in a novel way, provide argument/justification for the application
- Critique the historical impact on policy, writings, advances

### Create × DOK 2
- Brainstorm ideas, concepts, problems, or perspectives related to a topic, principle, or concept
- Generate testable conjectures or hypotheses based on observations, prior knowledge, and/or artifacts
- Synthesize information within one source or text
- Develop a complex model or symbol

### Create × DOK 4
- Develop/support an alternative solution
- Synthesize information across multiple sources or texts
- Articulate a new voice, alternate theme, new knowledge, or new perspective
- Create historical fiction drawing on sources

## Using the CRM for Item Classification

When assigning a Hess CRM cell to an item:

1. Identify the **Bloom's level** demanded by the cognitive task (not just the verb)
2. Identify the **DOK level** based on the depth of processing required
3. Find the intersection cell — if the cell is empty, the combination is either invalid or the item needs reclassification
4. Record the cell as `{Bloom's level} × DOK {level}` (e.g., `Analyze × DOK 3`)

### Common Misclassification Traps

| Trap | Example | Correct Classification |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| Using "analyze" verb but only requiring recall | "Analyze means identify in context" | Remember × DOK 1 |
| Labeling DOK 3 when only compare/contrast needed | "Compare the North and South economies" | Analyze × DOK 2 |
| Calling it DOK 1 when cause-effect reasoning needed | "Explain why the stock market crashed" | Understand × DOK 2 |
| Labeling DOK 2 when evidence-based argument required | "Use evidence to evaluate FDR's New Deal" | Evaluate × DOK 3 |
