# Webb's Depth of Knowledge — Social Studies Descriptors

Source: [Webb DOK for Social Studies (Hess, 2005)](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Testing/Student-Readiness-Toolkit/DOKsocialstudies_KH08.pdf.aspx)
Based on Webb (2002) and compiled by Karin Hess, National Center for Assessment.

## Critical Rule for Large-Scale Assessment

> Webb recommends that large-scale, on-demand assessments only assess DOK Levels 1, 2, and 3, due primarily to testing time constraints. DOK Level 4 in social studies is best reserved for local assessment.

**For TCAP items**: Only DOK 1, 2, and 3 are valid. Any item tagged DOK 4 must be flagged as `tcap_format: false` and labeled for classroom/local assessment use only.

## DOK Level Descriptors

### Level 1 — Recall of Information

Level 1 asks students to recall facts, terms, concepts, trends, generalizations, and theories or to recognize or identify specific information contained in graphics.

**Generally requires**: identify, list, define

**Typical prompts**: Who, what, when, where

**Note on describe/explain**: Items requiring "describe" or "explain" could be Level 1 or 2 depending on what is to be described/explained. A Level 1 describe/explain would recall, recite, or reproduce information.

**Map/chart/graph items**: Items requiring students to recognize or identify specific information contained in maps, charts, tables, graphs, or drawings are generally Level 1.

#### Level 1 Task Examples
| Code | Task |
|------|------|
| 1a | Recall or recognition of: fact, term, concept, trend, generalization, event, or document |
| 1b | Identify or describe features of places or people |
| 1c | Identify key figures in a particular context |
| 1d | Describe or explain: who, what, where, when |
| 1e | Identify specific information contained in maps, charts, tables, graphs, or drawings |

### Level 2 — Basic Reasoning

Level 2 includes the engagement of some mental processing beyond recalling or reproducing a response. Students contrast or compare people, places, events and concepts; convert information from one form to another; give an example; classify or sort items into meaningful categories; describe, interpret or explain issues and problems, patterns, reasons, cause and effect, significance or impact, relationships, points of view or processes.

**Key distinction from Level 1**: A Level 2 "describe or explain" requires students to go beyond a description or explanation of recalled information to describe or explain a result or "how" or "why."

#### Level 2 Task Examples
| Code | Task |
|------|------|
| 2a | Describe cause-effect of particular events |
| 2b | Describe or explain: how (relationships or results), why, points of view, processes, significance, or impact |
| 2c | Identify patterns in events or behavior |
| 2d | Categorize events or figures in history into meaningful groups |
| 2e | Identify and summarize the major events, problem, solution, conflicts |
| 2f | Distinguish between fact and opinion |
| 2g | Organize information to show relationships |
| 2h | Compare and contrast people, events, places, concepts |
| 2i | Give examples and non-examples to illustrate an idea/concept |

### Level 3 — Complex Reasoning

Level 3 requires reasoning, using evidence, and a higher level of thinking than the previous two levels. Students go beyond explaining or describing "how and why" to justifying the "how and why" through application and evidence.

**Key distinction from Level 2**: The cognitive demands at Level 3 are more complex and more abstract than Levels 1 or 2.

#### Level 3 Task Examples
| Code | Task |
|------|------|
| 3a | Explain, generalize, or connect ideas using supporting evidence from a text/source |
| 3b | Apply a concept in other contexts |
| 3c | Make and support inferences about implied causes and effects |
| 3d | Draw conclusions or form alternative conclusions |
| 3e | Analyze how changes have affected people or places |
| 3f | Use concepts to solve problems |
| 3g | Analyze similarities and differences in issues or problems |
| 3h | Propose and evaluate solutions |
| 3i | Recognize and explain misconceptions related to concepts |
| 3j | Analyze and explain multiple perspectives or issues within or across time periods, events, or cultures |

### Level 4 — Extended Thinking (Local Assessment Only)

Level 4 requires the complex reasoning of Level 3 with the addition of planning, investigating, or developing that will most likely require an extended period of time. The extended time period is not a distinguishing factor if the required work is only repetitive and does not require applying significant conceptual understanding and higher-order thinking.

**NOT valid for TCAP items.** Use for classroom assessment, performance tasks, and projects only.

#### Level 4 Task Examples
| Code | Task |
|------|------|
| 4a | Analyze multiple sources of evidence across time periods, themes, issues |
| 4b | Gather, analyze, organize, and synthesize information from multiple (print and non-print) sources |
| 4c | Make predictions with evidence as support |
| 4d | Plan and develop solutions to problems |
| 4e | Given a situation/problem, research, define, and describe the situation/problem and provide alternative solutions |
| 4f | Describe, define, and illustrate common social, historical, economic, or geographical themes and how they interrelate |

## Source of Challenge Criterion

The Source of Challenge criterion identifies items where the major cognitive demand is inadvertently placed on something other than the targeted social studies content. Excessive reading demands, cultural bias, or specialized knowledge could cause students to answer incorrectly even though they have the required social studies knowledge.

**Items with an appropriate source of challenge** differentiate between students who have the social studies knowledge being assessed and those who do not.

## DOK as Ceiling, Not Target

Use the highest DOK level implicit in a standard as the ceiling for assessment, not the target. A standard with a DOK 3 ceiling can be assessed at DOK 1, 2, or 3 — not only at DOK 3. This prevents:

1. The assessment as a whole becoming too difficult
2. Loss of important information about student learning along the achievement continuum
