## Core Expert Content

### 1. The Claim Hierarchy — Use This Every Time

Before writing any research-linked sentence, classify it:

```
LEVEL A — Design Rationale (always permissible)
  "History Hack is designed consistent with Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction,
   specifically the use of frequent low-stakes review questions."
  Source needed: cite the principle, not the product.

LEVEL B — Logical Alignment (permissible with care)
  "Spaced repetition research suggests that distributed practice improves
   long-term retention; History Hack's unit structure spaces review across
   multiple sessions to apply this principle."
  Source needed: cite the research, then describe the design feature separately.

LEVEL C — Efficacy Claim (NEVER without a study)
  "History Hack raises TCAP scores." — PROHIBITED until a study exists.
  "Students using History Hack outperform peers." — PROHIBITED.
  Workaround: "A pilot study is planned to evaluate whether History Hack
   improves TCAP EOC performance."
```

### 2. Evidence-Base Frameworks — Quick Reference

Load `references/research-foundations-outline.md` for the full annotated outline. Summary:

**Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction (2012)**
- 17 principles derived from cognitive science and master-teacher studies
- Most applicable to History Hack: daily review (#1), new material in small steps (#2), asking many questions (#3), scaffolds (#11), independent practice (#16)
- Cite: Rosenshine, B. (2012). Principles of Instruction. *American Educator, 36*(1), 12-19.

**Retrieval Practice / Testing Effect**
- Roediger & Karpicke (2006); Dunlosky et al. (2013) — retrieval is among the highest-utility learning strategies
- History Hack's question bank activates retrieval; this is a design-rationale claim, not an efficacy claim
- Cite: Dunlosky, J., et al. (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14*(1), 4-58.

**Spaced Repetition**
- Cepeda et al. (2006) — distributed practice consistently outperforms massed practice
- Applicable to multi-unit review cadence; NOT to in-session question repetition algorithms (those require real calibration data)

**Mayer's Multimedia Learning Principles (2009)**
- Coherence, signaling, segmenting, modality, and redundancy principles
- Applicable to any visual/text pairing in History Hack materials

**Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, van Merrienboer)**
- Germane vs. extraneous load; scaffolding; worked examples
- Relevant to lesson-chunk sizing and scaffold design

**Universal Design for Learning (CAST UDL Framework)**
- Three principles: multiple means of representation, action/expression, engagement
- Apply when writing accessibility rationale; coordinate with `accessibility-qc-agent`

**Culturally Responsive Pedagogy (Gay, 2000; Ladson-Billings, 1995)**
- History Hack's veteran/military representation and diverse historical perspectives can be framed here — but only describe what the curriculum actually contains (verify with `historian-factcheck-agent`)

### 6. Efficacy Study Design — Pilot a Solo Founder Can Actually Run

Read `references/pilot-study-design.md` for the full protocol template. Key principles:

**Realistic design for Year 1:**
- Design type: Single-group pre-post with historical comparison (Tier 3 attempt) or matched-school quasi-experimental (Tier 2 attempt)
- Sample: 1-2 partner schools, 2-4 classrooms per school, ~60-120 students minimum for a meaningful signal
- Duration: One full semester aligned with TCAP EOC administration cycle
- Primary outcome: TCAP U.S. History EOC score (obtain via district data-sharing agreement — coordinate with `tt-legal-team` for DPA)
- Secondary outcomes: Formative quiz gains within platform, teacher satisfaction survey
- IRB: If university partner is involved, require IRB approval; if district-only, require district research approval and parent/guardian consent. Defer all legal specifics to `tt-legal-team`.

**Sample size reality:**
- 60 students in a pre-post design gives ~80% power to detect a medium effect (d = 0.5) at alpha = 0.05
- A small effect (d = 0.2) requires ~200+ students — unrealistic for Year 1; be honest about this
- State: "This pilot is designed to generate preliminary evidence, not definitive proof of efficacy."

**Confound list to address in write-up:**
- Teacher effect (same teachers pre/post vs. different)
- Selection bias (volunteer schools tend to be high-implementing)
- Curriculum exposure time (dosage measured by platform logs)
- Prior achievement (use prior-year TCAP scores as covariate)
