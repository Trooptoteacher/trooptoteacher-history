## What's new in v2

This version adds four things based on classroom feedback:

1. **Primary-source imagery throughout.** The deck now shows the actual documents, period photographs, portraits, maps, and political cartoons — every image source-captioned and public-domain (Library of Congress, National Archives, and other public archives). 2. **Click-to-reveal TCAP checks.** Every "Check for Understanding" is now a **two-slide pair**: the question and answer choices appear first so students can attempt it, then the next click reveals the correct answer plus the "Why" explanation.

The US.02 (Dawes Act) standard was also reframed to be historian-grade and TCAP-neutral: students analyze the documented intent and consequences of federal assimilation policy from the evidence, rather than being handed a conclusion.

## How the deck is built

The deck opens with three unit-level slides — a **Cover**, a **Unit Agenda** (roadmap of all 7 standards), and a **Unit Hook** (a "Turn & Talk" bell-ringer students return to at the end).

Then **each standard (US.01–US.07) runs as a complete lesson** in the same repeating sequence, so students always know where they are:

1. **Standard Divider** — the standard code is highlighted alongside the official TN academic standard text and the "I Can" objective (gold bar).
2. **Hook** — a short provocation or quote with a discussion prompt.
3. **Direct Instruction** — chunked content slides with progress dots and period imagery; Tennessee Connection call-outs appear where relevant.
4. **Key Figures** — biography cards (where the standard has them).
5. **Primary Source Analysis** — a verified excerpt shown with the actual document/photo, full context, an analyze question, the Social Studies Practice it targets, and a clickable source link.
6. **Word Wall** — key vocabulary with the Spanish term shown for EL support.
7. **Student Activity** — a short collaborative task with grouping, deliverable, and entry/honors differentiation, paired to a Cornell Notes printable.
8. **Check for Understanding (two slides)** — slide 1 shows a TCAP-style question and choices for students to attempt; slide 2 reveals the correct answer and the "Why."
9. **Wrap-Up / Exit Ticket** — "What You Need to Know for TCAP" key takeaways plus a written exit ticket.

The deck closes by returning to the unit hook so students answer the opening question with evidence.

## Running the click-reveal in class

The TCAP checks are built as a **two-slide reveal** (no animation timing to configure — it works in both PowerPoint and Google Slides):

1. Advance to the **question slide** and let students answer (think-pair-share, whiteboards, or hands).
2. Click once to advance to the **reveal slide**, which shows the correct choice highlighted in gold plus the "Why" explanation.

This works identically when the .pptx is imported into Google Slides — because it's a slide advance, not an entrance animation, nothing breaks in conversion.

## The three answer keys

You can get the key in whichever form fits the moment:

- **Speaker notes** — open the Notes pane under any check slide for the answer and rationale while you present.
- **Reveal slides** — the in-deck reveal shows students the answer and the "Why."
- **Printable Teacher Answer Key PDF** (`Unit1_Teacher_Answer_Key.pdf`) — an 8-page branded document with, for every standard: the TN standard and "I Can" objective, the check item with the correct answer and explanation, the primary-source analyze prompt with a suggested answer, the student-activity expected deliverable, and an exit-ticket model answer. Print it for a sub, a co-teacher, or your own grading. It carries the same brand and a closing sourcing/verification note.

## Editing the deck

- **Fonts:** The deck uses **Georgia** (headlines) and **Calibri** / **Trebuchet MS** (body and labels) — all standard system fonts, so it renders correctly on any school computer without installing anything.
- **Colors:** History Hack navy `#1A2332`, red `#C62828`, gold `#C9A84C` / `#F9A825`, warm cream `#F7F4EC`, with an America250 deep blue `#002858` accent on primary-source slides.
- To adjust content, edit directly in PowerPoint — every text box is editable. To edit a check's answer, update **both** the reveal slide and the speaker note so they stay in sync.

## PowerPoint vs. Google Slides — recommendation

This deck is delivered as **PowerPoint (.pptx)** as the single source of truth, for two reasons:

1. **PowerPoint → Google Slides imports cleanly.** Any teacher who prefers Google Slides can upload the .pptx to Google Drive and open it with Google Slides (or **File → Import slides**). Layout, colors, images, and the two-slide reveal all carry over because the deck uses only standard system fonts and slide advances (not fragile animations).
2. **The reverse (Google Slides → PowerPoint) is messier** — exports often shift spacing, break shapes, and lose fidelity. Building in PowerPoint keeps the master version pristine.

**For distribution to other educators:** keep the .pptx as your master, and let teachers either (a) use it directly in PowerPoint, or (b) upload it to Google Drive and convert to Google Slides for editing. If you want a permanently shareable Google Slides link, upload once to Drive, convert, and share that copy — but continue making structural edits in the .pptx master so future units stay consistent.
