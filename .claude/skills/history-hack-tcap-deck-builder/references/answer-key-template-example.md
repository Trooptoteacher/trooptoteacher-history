## US.01 — Homestead Act and Transcontinental Railroad

**TN Standard:** US.01 – Explain how the Homestead Act and the Transcontinental Railroad impacted the settlement of the West.

**"I Can" objective:** I can explain how the Homestead Act and the Transcontinental Railroad impacted the settlement of the West, including their effects on American Indians, immigrants, and settlers.

### TCAP-Style Check for Understanding

> According to the Homestead Act, who was eligible to claim public land?

- **A.** Adult citizens or those intending to become citizens who headed families  ← **CORRECT**
- **B.** Only wealthy landowners who could afford to purchase it
- **C.** Only military veterans of the Civil War
- **D.** Railroad companies and large corporations

**Answer: A.** The Act specifies eligibility as "the head of a family" or someone "twenty-one years" old who is a citizen or "filed his declaration of intention to become such."

### Primary Source Analysis

**Source:** Homestead Act — U.S. Congress, May 20, 1862 (12 Stat. 392)

**Analyze prompt:** According to the Homestead Act, who was eligible to claim public land?

**Suggested answer:** The Act specifies eligibility as "the head of a family" or someone "twenty-one years" old who is a citizen or "filed his declaration of intention to become such."

### Student Activity — Cause-and-Effect Web: Tying the Nation Together

- **Grouping / Time:** Pairs · 10–12 min
- **Task:** Using the railroad map and the Golden Spike photo, list THREE effects of the Transcontinental Railroad on (a) settlers, (b) immigrant labor, and (c) American Indian nations. For each effect, mark whether it was a benefit or a cost — and to whom.
- **Expected deliverable:** Completed cause-effect web with at least 6 labeled effects.
- **Differentiation:** Entry: sentence stems provided. Honors: rank the three groups by net impact and defend the order.
- **Companion printable:** Cornell Notes — US.01: Transcontinental Railroad & Industrial Expansion (Teacher Tools › Worksheets › Cornell Notes)

### Wrap-Up — "What You Need to Know for TCAP"

**Key takeaways students should be able to name:**

- Sequence the expansion of the United States
- Impact on Western Settlement
- Impact on American Indians, Immigrants, and Settlers

**Exit-ticket model answer:** A strong exit ticket restates the "I Can" objective in the student's own words and uses at least one key term from the lesson (for example, *Homestead Act*). Accept any accurate paraphrase of the objective that includes a correctly used unit term.

## US.02 — Federal Policies Toward American Indians

**TN Standard:** US.02 – Examine federal policies toward American Indians, including the movement to reservations, assimilation, boarding schools, and the Dawes Act.

**"I Can" objective:** I can examine federal policies toward American Indians, including the movement to reservations, assimilation, boarding schools, and the Dawes Act.

### TCAP-Style Check for Understanding

> What was the main purpose of the Dawes Act based on this excerpt?

- **A.** To expand reservation lands for tribal use
- **B.** To return lands to Native American control
- **C.** To establish new tribal governments
- **D.** To divide tribal lands into individual private property  ← **CORRECT**

**Answer: D.** The Act authorized dividing reservation land “in severalty” (individual private ownership) and allotting 160 acres to each head of family, breaking up communal tribal ownership. The other options describe the opposite of what the text directs.

### Primary Source Analysis

**Source:** Dawes General Allotment Act — U.S. Congress, February 8, 1887 (24 Stat. 388)

**Analyze prompt:** What was the main purpose of the Dawes Act based on this excerpt?

**Suggested answer:** The Act authorized dividing reservation land “in severalty” (individual private ownership) and allotting 160 acres to each head of family, breaking up communal tribal ownership. The other options describe the opposite of what the text directs.

### Student Activity — Claim & Evidence: What Did the Policy Do?

- **Grouping / Time:** Pairs → Whole class · 12–15 min
- **Task:** Examine the Tom Torlino before/after photographs and the Dawes Act excerpt. In your own words, write what each policy CLAIMED to do and what it ACTUALLY did, citing one piece of evidence for each. Then take a position: was federal assimilation policy primarily about land, about culture, or both? Support your claim with evidence from today's sources.
- **Expected deliverable:** A claim-evidence-reasoning (CER) paragraph with at least two cited sources.
- **Differentiation:** Entry: T-chart (Claimed | Actually) instead of paragraph. Honors: evaluate the term “cultural genocide” — is it supported by the evidence? Why or why not?
- **Companion printable:** Cornell Notes — US.02: Federal Policies Toward American Indians (Teacher Tools › Worksheets › Cornell Notes)

### Wrap-Up — "What You Need to Know for TCAP"

**Key takeaways students should be able to name:**

- Movement to Reservations
- Assimilation and Boarding Schools

## US.03 — Compromise of 1877, Jim Crow & Plessy v. Ferguson

**TN Standard:** US.03 – Explain the impact of the Compromise of 1877, including Jim Crow laws, lynching, disenfranchisement methods, the efforts of Benjamin "Pap" Singleton and the Exodusters, and the Plessy v. Ferguson decision. (TCA)

**"I Can" objective:** I can explain the impact of the Compromise of 1877, including Jim Crow laws, lynching, disenfranchisement methods, the efforts of Benjamin "Pap" Singleton and the Exodusters, and the Plessy v. Ferguson decision.

### TCAP-Style Check for Understanding

> What argument did the Supreme Court make to justify segregation in Plessy v. Ferguson?

- **A.** Segregation was only temporary until equality could be achieved
- **B.** The Constitution required racial separation
- **C.** Separate facilities did not imply racial inferiority  ← **CORRECT**
- **D.** African Americans preferred separate facilities

**Answer: C.** The Court argued that separation laws "do not necessarily imply the inferiority of either race" and rejected the idea that segregation "stamps the colored race with a badge of inferiority."

### Primary Source Analysis

**Source:** Plessy v. Ferguson Decision — Supreme Court of the United States, May 18, 1896 (163 U.S. 537)

**Analyze prompt:** What argument did the Supreme Court make to justify segregation in Plessy v. Ferguson?

**Suggested answer:** The Court argued that separation laws "do not necessarily imply the inferiority of either race" and rejected the idea that segregation "stamps the colored race with a badge of inferiority."

### Student Activity — Reading a Political Cartoon as Evidence

- **Expected deliverable:** Annotated cartoon analysis with point-of-view statement.
- **Differentiation:** Entry: guided symbol list. Honors: compare Nast's view to the photo of the SC legislature — do the sources corroborate?

**TN Standard:** US.04 – Analyze the causes and consequences of Gilded Age politics and economics as well as the significance of the rise of political machines, major scandals, civil service reform, and the economic difference between farmers, wage earners, and industrial capitalists, including: Boss Tweed, Thomas Nast, Credit Mobilier, Spoils system and President James A. Garfield's assassination, Pendleton Act, Interstate Commerce Act.

**"I Can" objective:** I can analyze the causes and consequences of Gilded Age politics and economics, including political machines, major scandals, civil service reform, and economic differences between farmers, wage earners, and industrial capitalists.

### TCAP-Style Check for Understanding

> What problem was the Pendleton Act designed to address?

- **A.** Political pressure and forced contributions from government employees  ← **CORRECT**
- **B.** Low wages for government workers
- **C.** Lack of qualified government workers
- **D.** Corruption in private businesses

**Answer: A.** The Act prohibited requiring political contributions from government workers and banned using "official authority or influence to coerce the political action of any person."

### Primary Source Analysis

**Source:** Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act — U.S. Congress, January 16, 1883

**Analyze prompt:** What problem was the Pendleton Act designed to address?

**Suggested answer:** The Act prohibited requiring political contributions from government workers and banned using "official authority or influence to coerce the political action of any person."

## US.07 — Immigration: Old vs. New Immigrants

**TN Standard:** US.07 – Describe the differences between "old" and "new" immigrants, analyze the assimilation process for "new" immigrants, and determine the impacts of increased migration on American society, including: Angel Island, Ellis Island, Push and pull factors, Ethnic clusters, Jane Addams, Competition for jobs, Rise of nativism, Jacob Riis, Chinese Exclusion Act and Gentleman's Agreement.

**"I Can" objective:** I can describe the differences between "old" and "new" immigrants, analyze the assimilation process for "new" immigrants, and determine the impacts of increased migration on American society.

### TCAP-Style Check for Understanding

> What was the stated justification for the Chinese Exclusion Act?

- **A.** Chinese workers were taking jobs in government
- **B.** China had declared war on the United States
- **C.** Chinese immigrants refused to learn English
- **D.** Chinese laborers supposedly endangered order in certain areas  ← **CORRECT**

**Answer: D.** The Act claims "The coming of Chinese laborers to this country endangers the good order of certain localities," using this as justification for exclusion.

### Primary Source Analysis

**Source:** Chinese Exclusion Act — U.S. Congress, May 6, 1882 (22 Stat. 58)

**Analyze prompt:** What was the stated justification for the Chinese Exclusion Act?

**Suggested answer:** The Act claims "The coming of Chinese laborers to this country endangers the good order of certain localities," using this as justification for exclusion.

- **Task:** Examine Jacob Riis's “Five Cents a Spot” photograph and the Ellis Island inspection-room image. Write one sentence from the perspective of a new immigrant and one from a nativist worried about change. Then explain what Riis wanted viewers to FEEL and DO.
- **Expected deliverable:** Two perspective sentences + a photographer's-purpose statement.
- **Differentiation:** Entry: sentence stems for both perspectives. Honors: evaluate whether a staged photo is reliable evidence.
- **Companion printable:** Cornell Notes — US.07: Immigration & Urbanization
