A4
TCA § 49-6-1019 Compliance — Attorney Review
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC materials are designed for compliance with TCA § 49-6-1019's prohibited concepts provisions — SOAPS analysis frames sources as objects of historical inquiry rather than advocacy; [MULTIPLE PERSPECTIVES] flags identify contested interpretations; no content attributes collective responsibility by race or sex. However, design intent does not constitute legal compliance. An attorney has not reviewed Units 1, 7, and 8 — the units where content touching race, inequality, and political conflict is most concentrated — against the statute's specific prohibitions and the applicable implementing rules.

The operational consequence: TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC cannot represent to any district that these materials comply with § 49-6-1019. Any district that adopts materials without this review and subsequently faces a complaint under § 49-6-1019 has no documented vendor due-diligence to rely on. That creates liability for the district and reputational risk for TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

A Tennessee Schedule F reviewer assessing Table 4 Accessibility in the context of § 49-6-1019 would need to see: (1) evidence that the materials were reviewed against the statute's specific prohibited categories by someone with the legal and educational expertise to make that determination; (2) documentation that the review identified no prohibited content, or that any flagged content was revised; and (3) a consistent methodological approach — such as the SOAPS framework — that demonstrates the instructional treatment of difficult historical content is analytical rather than advocacy-based. The reviewer does not need a formal legal opinion in hand during the review, but the publisher should be able to produce one if challenged.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content
  • Identify every passage in Units 1, 7, and 8 that makes a factual claim about race, inequality, racial violence, or political protest. Flag each for attorney review.
  • For each primary source that involves racial content, confirm the SOAPS analysis questions are framed analytically — the student is asked to evaluate the source's perspective, not adopt it.
  • Confirm that no student activity, assessment prompt, or discussion question in these units asks students to identify with a racial or ethnic group's historical experience in a way that could be read as assigning current guilt or responsibility.
  • After attorney review: implement any specific language revisions the attorney recommends. Document the revision in a change log with the date and basis for each change.
Documentation
  • Prepare a single-page "Prohibited Concepts Compliance Summary" for TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC company records. This document should: identify the units reviewed; list the reviewing attorney's name, bar number, and date of review; summarize the finding; and note any specific revisions made as a result.
  • Add a statement to the front matter of the materials: "TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC has taken steps to ensure that all instructional content is consistent with applicable Tennessee law. Instructional treatment of all historical content is factual, multi-perspective, and does not promote or require student adoption of any viewpoint based on group identity."
  • File the attorney's written opinion with the company's legal records. This is not for distribution — it is documentation of due diligence.
External (Attorney)
  • Contact: Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts, Arts & Business Council of Greater Nashville · (615) 254-0030 · vlaa.org
  • What to request: A written legal opinion reviewing Units 1, 7, and 8 of the instructional materials against TCA § 49-6-1019 and applicable State Board implementing rules. Specifically: do any passages in these units constitute instruction that promotes prohibited concepts as defined by the statute?
  • Deliverable required: Written opinion. Oral advice is not sufficient — a written opinion is required for company legal records and to demonstrate due diligence to any district reviewing the materials.
  • Critical path note: This is the single longest-lead-time item in Phase 1. Schedule the appointment before any other Phase 1 action. Do not wait for G1 or C5 to be complete first.

A2
Digital Accessibility — WCAG 2.1 AA Compliance
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

The materials are digitally available with print functionality and work on any device without special software — these elements score positively. The gap is that no formal accessibility audit has been conducted, and two structural issues are known to fail WCAG 2.1 AA without an audit confirming otherwise: (1) SVG charts that convey data without tabular equivalents are not readable by screen readers — a student using assistive technology cannot access the unemployment rate data or bank failure data charts; and (2) images using JavaScript onerror fallback handlers may not provide meaningful alt text to screen readers depending on how the fallback is implemented.

The accessibility target for all web-delivered content is WCAG 2.1 AA. This is the standard referenced in federal educational accessibility guidance and the standard a Tennessee reviewer with accessibility expertise will apply. The verification method is WAVE automated testing (wave.webaim.org) plus manual checks for items WAVE does not detect — including SVG text equivalents, focus order under keyboard navigation, and screen reader compatibility with dynamic content. Both automated and manual checks are required; WAVE alone is not sufficient to confirm full WCAG 2.1 AA compliance.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer needs to confirm: (1) all lessons are available in digital form with a printable option — currently met; (2) every image has descriptive alt text accessible to screen readers; (3) data visualizations have a text-based equivalent (table or descriptive paragraph) so a student using a screen reader receives the same information as a sighted student; (4) color is not the sole means of conveying information (the red/green color coding in charts must have a text label as well); and (5) keyboard navigation works for all interactive elements. A score of 2 requires no known WCAG 2.1 AA failures.

C. Required Changes
Platform / UX (Primary Fix)
  • Step 1 — WAVE audit plus manual checks: Run all 23 HTML files through the WAVE tool at wave.webaim.org (automated check). Document every Error and Contrast Error. Then conduct manual checks for: (a) focus order — tab through each file and confirm logical keyboard navigation; (b) screen reader test — open 3 representative unit files with a screen reader (NVDA or VoiceOver) and confirm SVG charts, images, and navigation are announced meaningfully; (c) confirm no information is conveyed by color alone without a text equivalent. Priority WAVE categories to fix: missing alt text (Error), missing form labels (Error), low contrast (Contrast Error), empty links (Error).
  • Step 2 — Alt text: Every <img> tag must have a descriptive alt attribute (not empty). Images used purely for decoration should have alt="" — content images must have a description that conveys what a sighted reader would understand from the image. The onerror fallback div should also include an aria-label attribute.
  • Step 3 — SVG chart data tables: After every SVG data chart, add a collapsed <details> element or a visible data table with the same data. Label it "Data table for [chart title]." This requires adding approximately 5–10 rows of data per chart — not a significant effort.
  • Step 4 — Color coding: Any information conveyed solely through color (red = bad year, green = good year) must also be conveyed through a text label, pattern, or icon. Add brief text labels to chart bars/lines that explain what the color indicates.
  • Step 5 — Keyboard navigation test: Tab through the index.html and three representative unit files to confirm all interactive elements (print button, navigation links) are reachable and operable by keyboard alone.
Documentation
  • After completing the audit and fixes: prepare a one-paragraph "Accessibility Compliance Statement" for the materials' front matter. State the accessibility target (WCAG 2.1 AA for all web-delivered content), the date of the audit, the tools and methods used (WAVE automated testing plus manual checks), and the finding. Do not claim full WCAG 2.1 AA compliance unless both automated and manual checks return no failures — claim "materials have been reviewed for WCAG 2.1 AA compliance using WAVE automated testing and manual verification."
  • Add a "Technology and Accessibility Requirements" section to the front matter. Contents: what devices and browsers are supported; how to enable print; a note that the materials are designed for compatibility with common screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) but have not been tested with every assistive technology configuration.
  • File the WAVE audit report (PDF or screenshot) with company records as documentation of due diligence.
I4
Formative Assessment — Tagging and In-Lesson Embedded Checks
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

Formative assessment mechanisms exist — spaced retrieval rounds, bell ringers in the learning science supplement, and lower-DOK question bank items usable for formative purposes. The score is 1 because: (1) the question bank does not distinguish between items designed for formative use (low-stakes, diagnostic, mid-lesson) and items designed for summative use (end-of-unit, graded); (2) no explicit formative check is embedded within the narrative text of individual lesson sections — a teacher reading through the narrative has no indicated stopping point for a comprehension check; and (3) the learning science supplement's bell ringers are housed in a separate file rather than embedded in the lesson sequence.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer needs to see frequent and varied formative assessment opportunities explicitly embedded within lessons — not only at the beginning (bell ringer) and end (exit ticket) of class periods, but at multiple points within the instructional sequence. At minimum: one explicit check-for-understanding per major section of narrative within each unit; a question bank with items tagged to indicate appropriate use (formative/diagnostic vs. summative); and teacher guidance that explains how to interpret formative results and adjust instruction in response. "Frequent and varied" in the Schedule F rubric means at least three distinct formative mechanisms per unit.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content
  • Add one explicit "Check for Understanding" prompt at the end of each major narrative section within every unit. This prompt should be 1–2 questions at DOK 1–2 that a teacher can use verbally or as a written quick-check. Format: a shaded box within the narrative labeled "Check for Understanding" with 1–2 questions and a brief note directing the teacher to the answer key.
  • These are not new assessment items — they can be drawn directly from existing question bank items. The change is positioning them within the lesson flow rather than leaving them in a separate question bank.
  • Confirm that the three spaced retrieval rounds per unit are explicitly described as formative assessment in the teacher guidance. Currently they function as formative assessment but are not labeled as such.
Platform / Tagging
  • Define the tagging schema first: Formative items = DOK 1–2, appropriate for mid-lesson or end-of-section check; Summative items = DOK 2–3, appropriate for end-of-unit graded assessment; Either = appropriate for both uses. Apply this schema to all 447 question bank items.
  • Add a "Use" filter to question-bank.html alongside the existing standard and DOK filters. Filter values: Formative | Summative | Both.
  • Update the question bank documentation to explain the tagging schema so that any teacher using the bank understands what the Use tag means.
Documentation
  • Add a "Formative Assessment Guide" section to the teacher guidance in the scope and sequence. Content: (a) explanation of how the three spaced retrieval rounds function as formative assessment; (b) how to use bell ringers and exit tickets from the learning science supplement; (c) how to use the Check for Understanding prompts embedded in the narrative; (d) how to read formative data and make pacing decisions.
  • This is a documentation addition to scope-sequence.html and the teacher guidance notes — no new content development required.
External
  • No external dependencies for I4. All required changes are within TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC's direct control.
A3
Teacher Support Clarity — Usability Validation and Priority Guidance
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

Teacher-facing guidance exists in every unit — pacing notes, example open-response answers, common misconceptions, and discussion protocol cards. The score is 1 because: (1) no independent teacher reviewer has confirmed that the guidance is actually usable without supplemental training; (2) no explicit "what to prioritize under time pressure" guidance exists — a teacher who reaches week 30 without having finished Unit 7 has no documented recommendation for what to compress or skip; and (3) teacher guidance is integrated into the same HTML files as student content, making it harder for a teacher to read through the guidance independently without navigating around student-facing materials.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer needs to confirm that teacher guidance is: (a) sufficiently clear and specific that a teacher new to the materials could implement a lesson without additional training; (b) organized so the teacher can find pacing, differentiation, and discussion guidance quickly; and (c) includes specific guidance for managing the three differentiation levels simultaneously in a single classroom. A reviewer testing the "Sunday night test" — could a first-year teacher open these materials on Sunday evening and know exactly what to do on Monday? — expects a clear lesson sequence, timing guidance, and flagged essential vs. optional elements.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content
  • Add a "Priority Teaching Guide" box to each unit's teacher section. Format: a three-row table. Row 1: "Essential — complete these regardless of time" (list 2–3 activities). Row 2: "Compress if needed" (list 1–2 activities that can be shortened). Row 3: "Skip if significantly behind" (list 1 activity that is enrichment rather than essential). This takes approximately 10 minutes per unit to write and requires no new content.
  • Add one sentence to the pacing note for each unit: "If this unit is running more than [X] days over estimate, use the Priority Teaching Guide above to adjust."
Documentation
  • A3 usability validation protocol: Recruit one qualified reviewer (Tennessee U.S. History teacher, Level 3 or above, not affiliated with TroopToTeacher Technologies LLC). Provide them with a five-question written checklist: (1) Could you identify the learning objective for this lesson within 2 minutes? (2) Could you prepare this lesson in under 25 minutes? (3) Did you know what to do if students finish early? (4) Did you know which version (A/B/C) to use for which students? (5) Was the discussion protocol clear enough to facilitate without additional research? Document their written responses. Revise any item receiving a "No" response.
  • File the completed checklist with company records as evidence of usability validation.
Platform / UX
  • Consider adding a "Teacher View" toggle to the unit HTML files that shows/hides teacher-only guidance sections. This separates teacher and student content in a single file without requiring two separate files. If this is not feasible within the current HTML structure, add a clearly labeled "Teacher Guidance" section at the top of each unit file with a visual separator from student content.
  • This is a usability improvement that directly addresses the reviewer concern about teacher guidance being buried within student-facing material.
External (Reviewer Recruit)
  • The independent reviewer is a low-barrier external dependency. Candidates: a colleague from your current school, a former cooperating teacher, or a district curriculum coordinator. The review should take approximately 1 hour per unit reviewed (suggest reviewing 2–3 units as a representative sample).
  • Timeline: can be scheduled within 1–2 weeks with minimal lead time.

Strong student-centered elements are present: concept-before-vocabulary launches in all units, Socratic seminar protocols in Appendix L, CER tasks requiring students to construct original claims, and the History Hack app's student-paced learning. The score is 1 because most student activities present a predetermined correct answer rather than a genuinely open investigative task, and collaborative inquiry — where students work together toward a shared conclusion rather than completing individual assignments — is described in protocols but not consistently structured into specific unit activities. A Schedule F reviewer applying the "student-centered learning" criterion looks for tasks that give students agency over the direction or product of their inquiry, not just choice among pre-structured options.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer needs to identify, in the actual student-facing materials, tasks that: (a) present students with an open question that does not have a single predetermined answer; (b) require students to select their own evidence to support a position; and (c) include at least some collaborative element where students negotiate or build on each other's thinking. This does not require project-based learning or multi-week investigations. A single well-designed inquiry task per unit — where students select two pieces of evidence and construct an argument the teacher cannot predict in advance — satisfies the spirit of this criterion. The key is that the student, not the curriculum, determines the direction of the inquiry within an appropriately scaffolded structure.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content (Primary Fix)
  • Add one "Open Inquiry Task" per unit. Format: a boxed activity within the unit student materials, 1–2 paragraphs. The task must: (a) present an open historical question (not answerable by recalling the textbook); (b) provide students with 3–4 source options from which they must select 2; (c) ask students to construct an argument using their chosen sources; (d) note that different students may reach different defensible conclusions. Example for Unit 5: "Was the New Deal the right response to the Great Depression? Select two of the following sources [list 4 sources] and construct a claim with evidence. There is more than one defensible position."
  • These tasks do not need to be new sources. Existing primary sources in the unit can serve as the evidence menu. The open question is the key innovation — reframe an existing activity around a question without a predetermined answer.
  • For collaborative structure: add a "Discuss with a partner" instruction to at least two existing think-pair-share prompts per unit. Specify: student A shares claim, student B responds with one piece of supporting or challenging evidence, both write a revised claim. This converts an existing individual activity into a collaborative one without new content.

P2
Synthesize Data Across Sources — Explicit Cross-Source Reasoning
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

Multi-source tasks exist — Unit 5 combines a data chart with a primary source; Unit 10 Version C includes a four-document DBQ. The score is 1 because the summative prompts in Units 1–9 do not explicitly require students to evaluate how sources corroborate, contradict, or complicate each other. A prompt that asks "Using the documents, explain the causes of the Great Depression" allows students to treat each document as an individual evidence item rather than engaging in synthesis. Synthesis — as defined by the Schedule F SSP.03 criterion — requires students to evaluate the relationship between sources: what does Source A tell us that Source B cannot, and why do they diverge?

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer looks for at least one task per unit where the prompt explicitly requires cross-source analysis — not just multi-source citation. The distinguishing language is: "Explain how Documents A and B corroborate or contradict each other's account of..." or "Document A and Document B offer different explanations. Which is better supported by the data in Chart C? Explain your reasoning." This language cannot be satisfied by summarizing each source individually. The reviewer would test a student response that summarizes each document without comparing them — a score of 2 requires that such a response would score poorly on the rubric because it does not meet the prompt requirement.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content (Copy Edit)
  • This fix is a targeted copy edit, not a content development task. For each of Units 1–9, locate the summative prompt (the DBQ or CER task). Revise the prompt to include one explicit cross-source directive.
  • Model revised prompt language: Add one of these sentence stems to each summative prompt where two or more sources are used: "In your response, explain how [Source X] and [Source Y] corroborate or contradict each other." / "Evaluate why [Source A] and [Source B] reach different conclusions about [topic]." / "Explain what the chart in Figure [X] reveals that the primary source in Document [Y] cannot."
  • Do not rewrite the entire prompt — add one targeted cross-source directive to the existing language. Estimated time: 15 minutes per unit × 9 units = 2–3 hours total.
  • Update the corresponding answer key to include a model response that demonstrates explicit cross-source synthesis rather than sequential source summary.
Documentation
  • Update the "Summative Assessment" section of Appendix K rubrics to include an explicit synthesis criterion. Currently the rubric rewards evidence quality and reasoning. Add a row: "Cross-source reasoning: Student evaluates how sources corroborate, contradict, or complement each other (2 pts)." This makes the synthesis requirement explicit in the scoring instrument, not just in the prompt.

P4
Historical Awareness — Explicit Historical Thinking Skill Labels
SCORE: 1
A. Current Issue

Historical thinking skills are embedded throughout the materials. Causation analysis is present in Unit 5 (multi-cause analysis of the Depression) and Unit 10 (9/11 and financial crisis). Comparison across eras is present in Version B and C assessment items. Continuity and change are addressed in cross-unit synthesis questions. The score is 1 because these skills are embedded implicitly — students are practicing them without being told what disciplinary skill they are using. A Schedule F reviewer assessing SSP.05 (Develop Historical Awareness) looks for evidence that students are learning the discipline's vocabulary and conceptual framework, not just the content. Materials that teach historical thinking without naming it score 1.

B. What a Reviewer Needs to Justify a Score of 2

The reviewer looks for explicit naming of historical thinking skills in the student-facing materials. The specific skills the Tennessee standards and Schedule F rubric expect to see labeled are: Chronological Reasoning; Causation (proximate vs. structural); Comparison (across time, place, or group); Continuity and Change Over Time (CCOT); and Contextualization (situating an event within its broader historical moment). These do not need to appear in every activity — but they must appear consistently enough that a student completing these materials would leave the course knowing these terms and what they mean as disciplinary tools.

C. Required Changes
Curriculum / Content (Copy Edit)
  • Add historical thinking skill labels to activity headers. Format: a small label in the activity header alongside the DOK badge. Use this set of terms: CAUSATION | COMPARISON | CCOT | CHRONOLOGY | CONTEXTUALIZATION. One label per activity. The label identifies the primary historical thinking skill the activity practices.
  • Minimum frequency: Each of the five skill labels must appear at least twice across the 10 units. No skill should appear more than four times — this is not a comprehensive taxonomy exercise, it is a consistent labeling practice.
  • This is a copy edit: add a one-word label to each of 20 activity headers across the 10 units. Estimated time: 2 hours.
  • Add a "Historical Thinking Skills Reference" to the front matter (one page). Define each term with a brief example from the course. This is the student-facing definition that allows the labels in activities to have meaning. Example: "CAUSATION — Identifying and evaluating the multiple causes of a historical event. Example: Analyzing the political, economic, and social causes of the Great Depression in Unit 5."
Documentation
  • Add the Historical Thinking Skills Reference to the "How to Use This Textbook" front matter section. Cross-reference it from the scope and sequence.
  • Update the scope-sequence.html to note which historical thinking skills are emphasized in each unit. This adds one row to the existing unit summary tables — no new content required.
  • Update the Standards Alignment Matrix to include a "Historical Thinking Skill" column. This helps a reviewer see at a glance that SSP.05 is addressed systematically across the course, not incidentally.
